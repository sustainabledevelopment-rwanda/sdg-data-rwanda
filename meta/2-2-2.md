national_indicator_description: Wasting, or low weight-for-height, is a measure of acute undernutrition. It represents a failure to receive ade- quate nutrition in the period immediately preceding the survey. Wasting may result from inadequate food intake or from a recent episode of illness or infection leading to weight loss. Overweight, or high weight-for-height, results from an imbalance between energy consumed (too much) and energy expended (too little).
national_geographical_coverage: Rwanda
computation_units: Percentage (%)
computation_definitions: This indicator is calculated as the percentage of children under five years old whose weight for height are less than minus two standard deviations (wasting) and greater than plus two standard deviations (overweight) from the median weight for height of the reference population ages 0–59 months. The nutritional status of children in the survey population was compared with the 2006 WHO Child Growth Standards (WHO, 2006) 
computation_calculations: (Number of children under age five that fall below minus two standard deviations or above plus two standard deviations from the median weight for height of the 2006 WHO Child Growth Standards) / (Total number of children under age five in sample) * 100

Child wasting refers to a child who is too thin for his or her height and is the result of recent rapid weight loss or the failure to gain weight. A child who is moderately or severely wasted has an increased risk of death, but treatment is possible. Child wasting is one of the World Health Assembly nutrition target indicators.
